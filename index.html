Should Farm Boy sell this product in Vietnam?

The purpose of this product plan is to decide if a Canadian product should be sold in a new foreign market. To answer that, I looked at Vietnam's country facts, business culture, trade relations, Farm Boy as a company, the product itself, and a simple marketing plan.

Product

The product is Farm Boy Eda-Yummy Spicy Kale Edamame Dip, 275 g. I chose it because it is a real Canadian grocery product, it has a fresh and unique flavour, and it feels different from many common dips.

Country

The foreign country is Vietnam. I chose Vietnam because it is a large market, it has strong trade links with Canada, and it has a different culture, which makes it a good country to study for this project.

Purpose

This project is meant to show if the product and country are a good fit. It also shows what problems Farm Boy would need to solve before entering the market.

Summaries and Analyses

10 key pieces of information

1. Vietnam had a population of 100,987,686 people in 2024.

This shows Vietnam has a very large market. Farm Boy would only need a small group of buyers, and this population size gives the product room to find them.

2. About 40.2% of Vietnam's population lived in urban areas in 2024.

This is helpful because city shoppers are easier to reach in premium grocery stores. It supports starting in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City instead of trying to sell across the whole country right away.

3. Vietnam's GDP per capita was US$4,717.30 in 2024.

This shows the product would likely be too expensive for most shoppers. It makes more sense to sell it as a niche product for middle and upper income buyers who already purchase imported food.

4. Vietnam is a mixed economy that allows market activity and foreign trade while the state still guides some sectors.

This is a good sign because private business and foreign trade are allowed in Vietnam. It also means Farm Boy would need to follow rules carefully and work well with local partners.

5. Canada and Vietnam are both in the CPTPP, which helps reduce trade barriers and tariffs.

This makes it easier to bring a Canadian product into Vietnam. Lower trade barriers help keep costs down and make market entry more realistic.

6. Vietnam's business culture places strong value on hierarchy, respect, and relationship building.

This means Farm Boy cannot act too casual in this market. The company would need to be patient, respectful, and more formal when dealing with partners and retailers.

7. Farm Boy is a growing Canadian grocery company owned by Empire Company Limited.

This shows Farm Boy has a strong base in Canada and support from a much larger company. That makes a small test launch in another country feel more realistic.

8. The Eda-Yummy Spicy Kale Edamame Dip is a customer favourite in Canada and fits Farm Boy's fresh private-label brand.

It is safer to export a product that already does well in Canada. This gives Farm Boy a product with clear strengths before entering a new market.

9. In Vietnam, TerraSana hummus is a direct competitor and local chili sauces are indirect competitors.

This shows the product would face competition right away. Farm Boy would need to show why this dip is different by focusing on its taste, ingredients, and premium imported image.

10. The most practical distribution plan is refrigerated sea shipping through a Vietnam-based distributor into premium supermarkets.

This shows the product can be sold in Vietnam, but only if shipping and cold storage work properly. It also shows that distribution is the biggest risk because this is a chilled product.
